Separate the material finding from the building plan

For a Bexleyheath garden building, the first question is what has actually been identified: roof sheets, wall cladding, a lining inside, or more than one of these. A photograph can show the layout, but it cannot confirm asbestos content. If a survey names asbestos cement sheets, send that page and mark the matching building. If there is no result yet, explain whether a builder is about to repair a leak, replace only the roof or clear the whole structure. That distinction stops a roof quote being confused with demolition and disposal of a complete garage.

Access matters just as much as the sheet count. Tell us whether a vehicle can reach the building, whether the passage crosses a neighbour’s land and whether the roof joins another structure. Approximate length and width, safe ground-level pictures and a postcode are enough to start a conversation. Do not climb onto the roof or break material to get measurements.

Make the Welling flooring brief usable

A Welling renovation can expose several floor layers. The survey might identify tiles in one room, while the black adhesive below remains untested or is described separately. Quote from the exact schedule rather than assuming that every dark layer contains asbestos. Tell us which rooms are being lifted, whether kitchen units or other fixtures remain and when the new floor installer needs the space. If the job spans a hallway and two rooms, mark them individually on a simple plan or annotate the survey.

If you are buying rather than already in possession, say when access for assessment is available. We can talk through the difference between the confirmed removal scope and material that first needs sampling or a refurbishment survey. The planned flooring finish also matters: it helps explain what needs to be handed over to the next trade without promising a method before the material is identified.
